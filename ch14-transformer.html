1The idea of attention: where to look

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

To know what "it" refers to, which word of the sentence should be looked at? Can it be looked at directly, without passing step by step as in chapter 13?

RESULT: the head in GPT-2 small (12 layers × 12 heads) where "it" looks at "animal" the most

→ Meaning. Each word sets a share of "how much to look" at every earlier word (summing to 1) and mixes in information by those shares. However far away, it looks in one go. Other heads look at entirely different things (layer and head sliders).

One line to remember. Attention = each word decides how much to look at the other words and mixes their information. In one go, regardless of distance.

2Query, key, value and scaled dot-product attention

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

How are the "how much to look" shares calculated?

TERMS IN THIS SECTION

Q (query)
What I am looking for (the "question").
K (key)
Each token's label. A large dot product with Q means a good match.
V (value)
The content actually taken.
d_k
Length of the Q and K vectors.

WORKED EXAMPLE: 3 tokens, d_k = 2

Row 1 only: scores → divide by √2 → softmax → mix the values.

→ Meaning. Token 1's question (1, 0) matches key (2, 0) best, so it takes the most (57.6%) of token 1's value. The output is a weighted average of the values.

One line to remember. softmax(QKᵀ/√d_k)V: dot products of questions and labels → shares → a weighted average of contents.

3Self-attention and multi-head attention

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

Where do Q, K and V come from? How can several kinds of relation (grammar, reference, the previous word) be seen at once?

  • X one row per token (tokens × d)
  • W^Q_i, W^K_i, W^V_i head i's weights, size d × d/h
  • W^O weights that mix the joined result, d × d

In words: Q, K and V are made from the same sentence X with three sets of weights (self). Split d into h pieces, run attention separately in each head, and join the results. GPT-2 small: d = 768 and 12 heads → 64 per head.

WORKED EXAMPLE: one GPT-2 small layer, a sentence of 10 tokens

With d = 768 and h = 12, each head is 768 ÷ 12 = 64 wide. Follow the shapes.

Count the weights (without biases).

→ Meaning. 3 × 12 × 768 × 64 equals 3 × 768 × 768: splitting into 12 heads leaves the number of weights and the amount of computation the same as one head. Instead, the 12 heads look at different relations separately. Input and output have the same shape (10 × 768), so layers can be stacked.

RESULTS: GPT-2 small, the same sentence, out of 144 heads

→ Meaning. Each head has its own job: one looks only at the previous word, another finds what a word refers to. Many heads park their attention on the first token when there is nothing relevant to look at (attention sink): not a rule anyone set, but a habit that emerged in training.

One line to remember. Self-attention = Q, K and V from the same X. Multi-head = several viewpoints seen separately and joined.

4Positional encoding

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

Attention does not know order (shuffle the words and the shares are the same). How is position given to it?

  • pos the token's position (counted from 0)
  • PE(pos) the whole vector added at that position (d slots)
  • PE(pos, k) slot k of that vector, a single number (k = 0 to d − 1)
  • i the number of a pair of slots: slot 2i holds the sin, slot 2i + 1 the cos

WORKED EXAMPLE: the PE of positions 0, 1 and 2 with d = 4

With d = 4 the slots are k = 0, 1, 2, 3 and the pairs are i = 0 (slots 0, 1) and i = 1 (slots 2, 3). The denominator is 10000⁰ = 1 for i = 0 and 10000^(2/4) = 100 for i = 1.

One slot first: slot 2 of position 1 is the sin of pair i = 1.

Filling in all four slots gives one vector per position (angles in radians).

Compare the dot products of two pairs at distance 1 (0 and 1, 1 and 2).

→ Meaning. The first pair (slots 0, 1) turns fast, 1 radian per position (a second hand); the second pair (slots 2, 3) turns slowly, 0.01 radian per position (an hour hand). Just as both dot products equal cos 1 + cos 0.01 = 1.540, the dot product of two PEs depends only on their distance, not on where they are. At distance 2 it drops to cos 2 + cos 0.02 = 0.584. GPT-2 learns a vector per position instead of using this formula, yet nearby positions still end up similar (cosine similarity: positions 10 and 11 0.998, 10 and 40 0.64).

One line to remember. Positional encoding = add a position pattern to each token vector. Formula or learned, nearby positions are similar.

6Encoder, decoder and masking

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

When writing text, the model must not see words it has not written yet. How is that prevented?

WORKED EXAMPLE: row 2 with section 2's Q, K, V (token 2 may see only tokens 1 and 2)

The dot products of q₂ = (0, 1) with the three keys, divided by √2, are (0, 1.414, 0.707). Adding the mask turns only token 3's slot into −∞.

→ Meaning. Without the mask row 2 is (0.140, 0.576, 0.284), so it looks 28% at token 3, which has not been written yet. With the mask token 3's share is exactly 0 and the first two split the total of 1. When generating text, peeking at later words is ruled out.

One line to remember. Causal mask = set future cells to −∞ → 0 after softmax. Encoders see everything; decoders see only the past.

8LLMs: predicting the next token

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

What, in the end, has an LLM like ChatGPT learned?

  • x_{t+1} next token
  • h_t the vector the last layer gives at token position t (GPT-2: 768 numbers)
  • W a matrix that turns h_t into one score per kind of token (GPT-2: 50,257 × 768)
  • T temperature (as in chapter 13, section 5)

A token is a piece between a character and a word (BPE). GPT-2's token table is mostly English, so each Korean character breaks into 2–3 byte pieces (▢): 11 characters become 24 tokens. LLMs built for Korean use other token tables.

→ Meaning. Small GPT-2 knows grammar but little about the world ("Paris" is 5th). Today's LLMs are the same structure grown thousands of times larger, trained on far more data and polished with human feedback. Appending only the most likely token (greedy) gives: "Deep learning is a very powerful tool for learning about the world around us. It's a tool that can help us".

One line to remember. LLM = a causal Transformer trained on "guess the next token" at very large scale. Its output is probabilities; the temperature sets how to pick.
