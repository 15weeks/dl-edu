DL-EDU · CHAPTER 7

Optimizer

Mini-batches, optimizers and learning rate

What makes chapter 2's gradient descent fast and stable in practice: using the data a little at a time (mini-batches), building up speed (momentum), sizing steps per direction (RMSprop, Adam), shrinking steps over time (schedules), and how to choose all these settings.

1Training in mini-batches, and batch size

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

With a million samples, must every step add up a million gradients?

TERMS IN THIS SECTION

batch
The group of samples used for one step. B = its size.
SGD
stochastic gradient descent. GD that steps with randomly chosen batches.
epoch
One pass over all the data = N / B steps.
  • g_B the gradient measured on a batch (an estimate of the full gradient)
  • B small: many wobbly steps; large: few straight steps

WORKED EXAMPLE: N = 256

→ Meaning. Small batches are cheap and step often, but their direction wobbles (the wobble even helps a little against overfitting). On a GPU, 32–256 is common.
batch size

One line to remember. Mini-batch = estimate the full gradient from a batch and step often. Smaller batches wobble more; larger ones go straighter but cost more.

2SGD and momentum

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

How do we stop crawling along the gentle direction of chapter 2, section 8's long valley? Remember the speed, like a rolling ball.

  • v velocity: the accumulated past gradients
  • β how much it remembers (usually 0.9)
  • g this step's gradient
  • if the direction keeps repeating, speed builds up (like a rolling ball)

WORKED EXAMPLE: f = (x² + 50y²)/2, 40 steps from (−4.5, 1) with η = 0.035

→ Meaning. Same η, but momentum ends 90 times lower. Speed builds up along the gentle x direction. In exchange it sways along the steep y direction: with β = 0.9 it sways so much in this valley that it ends up slower.

One line to remember. Momentum = accumulate gradients into a velocity and step with it. Consistent directions speed up; too large a β sways.

3RMSprop and Adam

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

If each direction has its own steepness, can each direction get its own step size?

TERMS IN THIS SECTION

RMSprop
root mean square propagation. Divides by a running average of gradient size.
Adam
adaptive moment estimation. Momentum + RMSprop.
m̂, v̂
m and v corrected for their bias in the first few steps (they start at 0).
  • m average direction (momentum)
  • s, v average size: larger in steep directions → dividing makes those steps smaller
  • Adam defaults: η = 0.001, β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.999

WORKED EXAMPLE: the valley from section 2, 40 steps

→ Meaning. g/√s is roughly a step that keeps only the sign, so x and y shrink at the same rate. In this valley RMSprop is fastest and Adam overshoots a little because of its momentum. No optimizer wins every time.

One line to remember. RMSprop and Adam divide each direction's step by its gradient size; Adam adds momentum on top. The default for most deep learning.

5Adam + L2 and AdamW

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

Does chapter 5's L2 (weight decay) still work as intended when combined with Adam?

In words: with Adam + L2 the penalty is mixed into the gradient and divided by √v, so weights with large gradients get a small penalty. AdamW takes the penalty out and gives every weight the same one.

WORKED EXAMPLE: the figure's two weights, both w = 1, η = 0.001, λ = 0.01

Weight A has small gradients (size 0.01) and weight B large ones (size 1). √v̂ is the average gradient size, so take 0.01 for A and 1 for B. Compare how much the penalty shrinks w in each step.

→ Meaning. With Adam + L2, A (small gradients) shrinks 100× more than B. The gradient size, not λ, ends up setting the strength of the penalty. AdamW shrinks both by 0.001% (a factor 0.00001) per step, exactly as λ says.
torch.optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=1e-3, weight_decay=0.01)    # Adam + L2 (the penalty is divided by √v)
torch.optim.AdamW(model.parameters(), lr=1e-3, weight_decay=0.01)   # AdamW (penalty applied separately; today's default)

One line to remember. For weight decay with Adam, use AdamW. Transformers (chapter 14) are trained with AdamW too.

6Learning-rate decay and schedules

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

What shape should chapter 2, section 6's "big steps first, small steps later" take?

  • η₀ starting η
  • t current epoch
  • γ shrink factor
  • s step length: shrink every s epochs
  • η_min the η cosine ends at
  • T total epochs
  • warmup ramp up from 0 over the first few epochs (common with Adam and Transformers)

WORKED EXAMPLE: the figure's settings, η₀ = 0.1, T = 100 epochs

Step multiplies by γ = 0.5 every s = 25 epochs, exponential by γ = 0.97 every epoch, and cosine goes down to η_min = 0.001. Compute η at t = 10 and t = 50.

→ Meaning. Starting from the same 0.1, at the middle (t = 50) step gives 0.025, exponential 0.022 and cosine 0.051. Cosine stays large early on, then drops quickly at the end to settle at the bottom.
opt = torch.optim.SGD(model.parameters(), lr=0.1, momentum=0.9)
sched = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.CosineAnnealingLR(opt, T_max=100)   # StepLR, ExponentialLR also exist
for epoch in range(100):
    train_one_epoch(model, opt)       # calls opt.step() inside
    sched.step()                      # change η once per epoch

One line to remember. A schedule takes big steps early to move fast and small steps at the end to settle at the bottom. Cosine (+ warmup) is common today.

7How to choose metaparameters

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

η, batch size, β, λ, number of layers … there is a lot to choose. How do we search?

TERMS IN THIS SECTION

metaparameter
Also called a hyperparameter. A setting chosen by a person, not learned.
grid search
Try every point of a grid of values.
random search
Try random points within the ranges.
