Activation & Loss

Activation functions, loss functions and normalization

Three choices you make when building a network: the nonlinearity between layers (activation), how to measure wrongness (loss), and how to put numbers on a common scale (normalization). We compare, in pictures, which choices make training work.

1Activation functions: sigmoid, tanh, the ReLU family

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

Chapter 4 said a nonlinearity is a must. What shapes are there, and how do they differ?

TERMS IN THIS SECTION

ReLU
rectified linear unit. max(0, z).
ELU
exponential linear unit. eᶻ − 1 for z < 0.
GELU
Gaussian error linear unit. A smooth ReLU.
  • blue line = f(z)
  • green line = slope f′(z): the number multiplied in during backprop (chapter 3, section 6)

WORKED EXAMPLE: slopes at z = −2, 0, 2

→ Meaning. The sigmoid's slope is 0.25 at the very most. ReLU, when on, always has slope 1, so it does not shrink the gradient.

One line to remember. Hidden layers usually use the ReLU family (GELU in today's Transformers). The sigmoid is for output probabilities (0–1).

2Activation slopes and vanishing gradients

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

What does chapter 2, section 9's "gradient multiplied at every layer" look like in a real network?

In words: layer 1's gradient carries the f′ of every layer above it. Multiply a sigmoid's f′ ≤ 0.25 ten times and it vanishes.

RESULTS (10 hidden layers × 32, computed live in the browser)

→ Meaning. With ten sigmoid hidden layers, layer 1 learns a million times more slowly than layer 10. tanh and ReLU (with a suitable initialization, chapter 9) keep them comparable.
hidden layers

One line to remember. Sigmoid hidden layers in a deep network leave the input-side layers unable to learn. That is why the ReLU family became the default.

3Loss functions: MSE, BCE, cross-entropy

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

Why not use MSE for classification? How differently do the two losses punish the same prediction?

  • p = σ(z), the probability given to the answer 1
  • p(1 − p) the sigmoid's slope: near 0 when badly wrong (p ≈ 0)
  • in BCE this factor cancels out

WORKED EXAMPLE: the answer is 1 but p = 0.01 (confidently wrong)

→ Meaning. When it is most wrong, MSE barely corrects it (a push of 0.02). Cross-entropy pushes 50 times harder. So classification uses cross-entropy.

One line to remember. Regression: MSE. Classification: cross-entropy. MSE on a sigmoid or softmax output loses its gradient on the samples that are most wrong.

"…WithLogits" and CrossEntropyLoss compute the sigmoid or softmax inside the loss, more stably. So the model's last layer gets no activation (the score z, the logit, goes straight in).

One line to remember. Regression → raw output + MSE; classification → raw logits + CrossEntropyLoss / BCEWithLogitsLoss.

5Data normalization: z-score, min-max

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

Why does training slow down when the inputs have very different sizes (height 170 cm, eyesight 1.2)?

  • μ mean
  • σ standard deviation
  • after z-score: mean 0, SD 1. After min-max: 0–1
  • compute μ and σ on the training data only, and use the same values on the test data

WORKED EXAMPLE: linear regression on two features, x₂ five times larger than x₁

→ Meaning. (Steps to get within 1% of w*, η = 50% of the largest stable value.) Different sizes stretch the loss valley. η must be small enough for the steep direction, so progress along the gentle direction is a crawl (the zigzag of chapter 2, section 8).

One line to remember. Putting the input features on a common scale (z-score) rounds the loss valley, so GD goes straight and fast.

6Batch normalization

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

Can we rescale not just the inputs but the hidden layers' values too?

TERMS IN THIS SECTION

BN
batch normalization. A layer that rescales each batch to mean 0, variance 1.
γ, β
gamma, beta. Learned parameters that stretch (γ) and shift (β) the result again.
ε
epsilon. A tiny number added so we never divide by 0.
  • μ_B, σ²_B this batch's mean and variance
  • γ, β learned by the network: it can even undo the rescaling if that helps

WORKED EXAMPLE: batch = [2, 4, 6, 8]

→ Meaning. During training each batch uses its own statistics; during evaluation the running mean and variance collected over training are used (train/eval, chapter 5, section 6).

One line to remember. Batch norm = inside a layer, rescale each batch to mean 0, variance 1, then adjust with γ and β. Training gets faster and tolerates larger learning rates.

7Example data: wine quality

WHAT WE WANT TO KNOW

How much difference does normalization make on real data?

Eleven chemical measurements (acidity, sugar, alcohol …) and a taste score for 1599 red wines. A score > 5 counts as "good" (1): two-class classification. Source: Cortez et al., Decision Support Systems, 2009 (UCI Machine Learning Repository, CC BY 4.0).

RESULTS (PyTorch, 11-16-1, SGD η 0.01, 60 epochs, 5 seeds)

→ Meaning. Same network, same training; normalization alone adds 13 points. Total sulfur dioxide (SD 33) and density (0.002) differ in size by more than ten thousand times.

One line to remember. For tabular data, z-score every feature before training. It is the cheapest step with the biggest effect.
